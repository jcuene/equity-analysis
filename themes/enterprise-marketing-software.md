# Project Snowbird — Phase 2 Theme Research: Enterprise Marketing Software

*Summer 2026 Investment Thesis · One of three foundational themes · Prepared for quarterly maintenance*

## Snapshot

Enterprise marketing software has moved from "AI-enhanced" to "AI-defined" faster than the original thesis anticipated, and the theme is now damaged-but-bifurcated rather than simply broken. The category is still large and growing—martech software spend is roughly $200 billion annually in 2026 (Gartner-derived analysis via ValueAdd VC), inside a broader marketing-technology economy some analysts size above $670B when services are included—but growth is now redistributive: AI budgets are being harvested from incremental seats rather than added on top. Every incumbent (Salesforce, Adobe, HubSpot) has re-architected around AI agents and shifted pricing from seats toward consumption/outcomes. The early-2026 "SaaSpocalypse" repriced the sector on fears that agents shrink seat counts. The theme's central question has inverted from "which incumbents add AI best" to "which incumbents survive AI disintermediation." Data ownership, distribution control, and pricing-model agility now separate winners from value traps.

## The Theme, Stated and Explained

**Original thesis (recap):** Marketing tools for enterprises and SMBs will change rapidly over five years as AI is integrated and AI-native entrants emerge; winners and losers separate on pricing-model evolution, distribution, a move into services, and core-product innovation.

**Where the theme stands now — a sell-side framing.** The 2026 reality is more violent than "integration." The seat-based licensing model that underpinned two decades of SaaS compounding is being actively repriced. As enterprise AI agents absorb the labor that previously required licensed human users, the linkage between headcount growth and software revenue growth is breaking. IDC's *FutureScape: Worldwide Agentic AI 2026 Predictions* (October 2025) states that by 2028 pure seat-based pricing will be obsolete, "forcing 70% of vendors to refactor their value proposition into new models." Corroborating monetization data (Software Equity Group / Kyle Poyar's State of B2B Monetization) shows pure seat-based pricing falling from 21% to 15% of companies in a single year while hybrid consumption models grew from 27% to 41%.

This is the "AI-disruption inversion" the prior pressure-test flagged. In the original thesis, AI was a tailwind incumbents would monetize. In 2026, AI is simultaneously (a) a monetization vector and (b) an existential threat to the revenue base it is being sold into. Salesforce is the cleanest illustration: per its May 27, 2026 Q1 FY27 release, combined Agentforce + Data 360 ARR reached nearly $3.4 billion (up over 200% Y/Y), of which Agentforce alone was $1.2 billion (up 205% Y/Y)—yet Salesforce cut ~4,000 support roles (Benioff: support org went from ~9,000 to ~5,000), held engineering headcount flat at ~15,000 for two years explicitly citing AI productivity, and ran multiple additional 2026 layoff rounds. The company is monetizing the same automation that lets its customers buy fewer seats. Overall Salesforce growth has settled into the low-teens (FY26 revenue $41.5B, up ~10%), with organic reacceleration guided only for H2 FY27.

**Pricing-model evolution (the key battleground).** The seats-to-outcomes shift is now concrete and measurable:
- **Salesforce** runs three parallel Agentforce models: Flex Credits ($500 per 100,000 credits; a standard action = 20 credits/$0.10, voice = 30/$0.15), the legacy $2-per-conversation SKU, and per-user Agentforce licensing—plus a pure pay-per-resolution Help Agent ("no charge" if the customer walks away unhappy).
- **HubSpot** shifted Breeze agents to outcome-based pricing in April 2026: Customer Agent at $0.50 per resolved conversation, Prospecting Agent at $1 per qualified lead. Management frames the industry as moving from a "token maxing" era to a "value maxing" era.
- **monday.com** launched consumption-based pricing with its AI Work Platform; AI ARR doubled Q/Q to 17% of net new ARR in Q2 FY26.
- **Sprout Social** is introducing a hybrid Trellis model (user access + usage).

The strategic read (SaaStr and pricing analysts): nobody yet knows the "right" way to price agentic work, so running parallel models is optionality, not confusion. The risk is that consumption pricing makes revenue less predictable and, if agents deflect enough work, structurally smaller per outcome.

**Distribution shift.** Two distribution threats are live. First, frontier labs (OpenAI, Anthropic) are shipping agent products and connectors that could commoditize the low end—Adobe now distributes agents *inside* ChatGPT and Claude, expanding reach but risking its direct customer relationship. Second, AI-driven discovery ("GEO"—generative engine optimization) is displacing traditional SEO; McKinsey's October 2025 "new front door to the internet" analysis warns unprepared brands may see traditional-search traffic decline "anywhere from 20 to 50 percent" and projects ~$750B of US consumer spend flowing through AI-powered search by 2028. This directly threatens the inbound-marketing playbook HubSpot was built on (its blog organic traffic already fell sharply after Google's 2024 core updates), which is why HubSpot launched AEO (Answer Engine Optimization).

**Move into services / "service-as-software."** The category's center of gravity is shifting from selling tools humans use to selling outcomes agents deliver—bullish for vendors who can meter the work (Salesforce Agentforce, Zeta's Athena) and bearish for vendors whose value was primarily a system-of-record UI.

**Core-product innovation & the data moat.** The emerging consensus—shared by Goldman Sachs and Morningstar—is that AI is "a filter, not the death of marketing software." The dividing line is proprietary, real-time customer data. Vendors sitting on first-party data moats and applying AI on top (Zeta, Klaviyo, Salesforce Data 360, Adobe's AEP + Firefly IP indemnity) are positioned as "AI-adjacent" beneficiaries; thin "AI-wrapper" tools without data or workflow depth get commoditized. Jasper is the cautionary tale: revenue peaked at a ~$120M run-rate in November 2023, fell ~54% to roughly $55M in 2024, and was reported around $88M ARR in 2025 as ChatGPT ate its core copywriting use case—a ~20% internal cut from its $1.5B peak valuation.

**Market structure — players and buyers.** Leading enterprise platforms: Salesforce (Marketing Cloud/Agentforce), Adobe (CX Enterprise, formerly Experience Cloud), Oracle, SAP, and HubSpot (moving up-market from SMB). Specialists/mid-market: Klaviyo (B2C CRM), Braze (customer engagement), Zeta Global (AI marketing cloud), Sprout Social (social), monday.com (work platform), The Trade Desk (ad-tech DSP). AI-natives: Jasper, Writer, Copy.ai. Buyers are consolidating—CMOs want fewer platforms, unified data, and demonstrable ROI; the average enterprise runs 30–120 martech tools at ~33% utilization (Gartner), so consolidation is a tailwind for platform winners and a headwind for point solutions. The martech landscape itself hit "peak martech" at ~15,505 products in 2026, growing just 0.7% (chiefmartec).

## Thesis Status

**Confirmed since the theme was set:**
- AI integration is now universal and central—~90% of marketing orgs use AI agents somewhere in the stack; AI has become "core marketing infrastructure" (Jasper's 2026 State of AI report: 91% of teams use AI, up from 63% a year earlier).
- Pricing-model evolution—a named winner/loser axis in the original thesis—is happening as hypothesized, and faster; seats → consumption/outcomes is now the dominant industry motion.
- AI-native entrants emerged (Jasper, Writer, Copy.ai) but most have struggled to build durable moats against both frontier LLMs and incumbents, supporting the thesis that data/distribution/workflow depth separate winners.
- Vendor consolidation toward platforms with data moats is a real, observable buying behavior.

**What changed (the AI-disruption inversion):**
- The core change is that AI flipped from pure tailwind to double-edged sword: growth engine *and* cannibalization risk. The "SaaSpocalypse" (term coined by a Jefferies trader, February 2026) repriced the sector on fears that agents shrink seat counts; Atlassian reportedly showed its first-ever enterprise seat-count decline, and Salesforce, Adobe, and HubSpot all fell sharply.
- **Morningstar (analyst Dan Romanoff) downgraded moats** on six wide-moat software firms—including Adobe and Salesforce—from wide to narrow on March 13, 2026, citing "fears of AI-led disruptions of the per-seat licensing model. AI is likely becoming consumption-based, meaning revenue streams will be less predictable." Fair values were cut (Adobe to $380 from $560; Salesforce to $280 from $300; ServiceNow to $165 from $200). HubSpot's uncertainty rating was raised to Very High (it was already narrow-moat), and after Q2 its fair value was cut again materially. This is credible confirmation that the disruption risk is structural, not merely sentiment.
- **The Trade Desk** is the live bear case: Q2 2026 revenue growth collapsed to 3% ($715M) with Q3 guidance implying a ~12% decline; the stock fell ~75–80% over the year on "AI disintermediation" (walled gardens Google/Meta/Amazon embedding AI ad-buying), impression scarcity from AI search, and Kokai rollout/governance missteps (Publicis stopped recommending it). Consensus 2026 revenue was cut from ~$3.18B to ~$2.74B, and a wave of analyst downgrades (Raymond James, BMO, Arete to Sell) followed.
- The valuation reset is severe but uneven: Morningstar's Romanoff estimates the median software name he covers is ~30% undervalued ("that is pretty extreme"), calling 2025–26 "an AI bust, not a boom" for software even as AI indices soared.

**What a skeptic would say right now:**
- "You're calling this a growth theme, but the reference customers are cutting seats and the vendors are cutting staff. Consumption pricing is a euphemism for 'we no longer control our revenue.' If an agent resolves a ticket for $0.50 that used to require a $175/month seat, revenue per outcome collapses even if 'usage' rises."
- "The data-moat argument is comforting but unproven—Adobe distributing agents inside ChatGPT is the vendor voluntarily handing the customer relationship to the disintermediator. Zeta and Klaviyo raising guidance is nice, but their margins are compressing (Klaviyo non-GAAP gross margin down ~300bps to 73.4% on SMS carrier fees; Braze still GAAP-unprofitable with a Q1 FY27 operating loss of $27.5M)."
- "The 'AI is a filter' framing avoids the possibility the category re-rates permanently lower. IDC and Gartner both say seat pricing is dead by 2028; the real question is whether the terminal value of the revenue base is capped or negative."
- "61% of marketers call this the biggest disruption in 20 years but only 7% have embedded AI in ways that deliver measurable results—so today's ARR spikes may be experimentation budgets that don't recur."

**Net assessment.** The thesis is damaged but not broken—it has bifurcated into a "Great Bifurcation." Companies that (1) own proprietary first-party data, (2) can meter agentic work as *net-new* revenue, and (3) have moved decisively to hybrid/consumption pricing are positioned to convert AI from threat to expansion (Salesforce, Zeta, Klaviyo, Adobe on data/indemnity grounds, HubSpot if AEO/agents offset SEO decay). Companies whose moat was a UI, seat-based lock-in, or an intermediary position in a disrupted value chain (The Trade Desk's open-web DSP model; thin AI wrappers; point solutions) face genuine terminal-value risk. The theme remains investable for an aggressive, growth-oriented, non-income portfolio, but must be re-scoped from "AI adoption" to "AI defensibility."

## Company-Specific Implications (watchlist notes — theme context, not scores)

- **Salesforce (CRM):** Bellwether of the paradox. Agentforce/Data 360 momentum is real ($3.4B combined ARR) but overall growth is low-teens and layoffs validate the seat-cannibalization thesis. Morningstar: narrow moat, FV $280, "survives and probably thrives." Watch H2 FY27 organic reacceleration as the key falsifier.
- **HubSpot (HUBS):** Best pure-play on the pivot—agent adoption strong (Customer Agent ~72% resolution; agentic actions up 3x YTD; >55% of Pro Plus customers use agents/Breeze Assistant) with outcome pricing live, but the customer-growth outlook was cut and SEO-decay risk is acute. Morningstar uncertainty raised to Very High; stock down ~48–52% over the year. NRR stuck near ~102%.
- **Adobe (ADBE):** Data + IP-indemnity moat (Firefly trained on licensed content) is a genuine differentiator; CX Enterprise repositions it as the CX orchestration layer, and AI-first ARR more than tripled YoY (Firefly approaching ~$300M ARR). But moat downgraded to narrow, simultaneous CEO+CFO transitions create a governance vacuum, and freemium is suppressing near-term ARR. Deeply out of favor (~40% below Morningstar FV).
- **The Trade Desk (TTD):** Clearest bear-case realization—structurally challenged by walled gardens plus AI-search impression scarcity. Highest disruption risk on the watchlist.
- **Zeta Global (ZETA):** Strongest "AI-adjacent" data-moat story—Q2 revenue +43.5% to $442.8M, Athena adoption surging, Palantir/Snowflake/OpenAI partnerships, 20th consecutive beat-and-raise. Agency-mix margin timing is the watch item.
- **Klaviyo (KVYO):** "Autonomous B2C CRM" with a deep first-party data moat; ~24–28% growth and Composer/Customer Agent traction, but SMS-driven gross-margin compression and SMB exposure are risks. Acquired Agency (AI customer-success) in Q2.
- **Braze (BRZE):** Agentic rollout (BrazeAI Operator, Agent Console, Decisioning Studio GA in April 2026) driving a fourth straight quarter of organic acceleration—Q1 FY27 revenue $211M (+30% YoY), DBNRR 110%, FY27 guide ~$895–899M—but still GAAP-unprofitable (non-GAAP op. income ~$10.5M). Named wins include Subway and Regal Cinemas. Goldman initiated Buy on the orchestration thesis.
- **Sprout Social (SPT) / monday.com (MNDY):** Both executing AI pivots amid restructurings (SPT ~20% headcount cut targeting ≥$50M savings; MNDY ~20% cut targeting ~$100M savings) with decelerating growth. monday's consumption pricing and AI ARR (17% of net new, $1.5B total ARR) are early positives but top-line is slowing to mid-teens; SPT's Trellis is improving retention.

## Recommendations

1. **Re-scope the theme from "AI adoption" to "AI defensibility."** For each watchlist name, score three axes for the later per-stock rubric: (a) proprietary first-party data depth, (b) ability to meter agentic work as *net-new* revenue rather than cannibalized seats, (c) pricing-model agility (hybrid/consumption already live).
2. **Overweight the data-moat "AI-adjacent" cohort** (Zeta, Klaviyo, Salesforce Data 360, Adobe on indemnity/data grounds) where AI is more expansion than cannibalization—the theme's most defensible expressions for an aggressive portfolio.
3. **Treat The Trade Desk as the theme's highest-risk name**—where the bear case is actively winning. Require evidence of Kokai delivering measurable ROAS gains *and* stabilization of the open-web/CTV impression base before adding.
4. **Use the valuation reset opportunistically, not indiscriminately.** Morningstar's ~30% median undervaluation is real, but moat downgrades mean the required margin of safety is larger than pre-2026. Prefer names where consumption ARR is running ahead of GAAP revenue (a healthy transition signal) over names where seat counts are outright declining.
5. **Set explicit quarterly falsifiers/benchmarks:**
   - *Bullish confirmation:* Salesforce H2 FY27 organic reacceleration; HubSpot NRR inflecting above 102% with credit consumption compounding; consumption/outcome revenue growing faster than seat attrition at 2+ watchlist names; Zeta/Klaviyo sustaining 20%+ growth without further margin erosion.
   - *Bearish escalation:* Any watchlist name reporting an outright *decline* in seats or ARR; consumption revenue proving to be non-recurring experimentation; further Morningstar moat downgrades; frontier-lab agents demonstrably winning enterprise CX deals directly.

## Caveats

- **Market-sizing dispersion is extreme.** 2025 martech estimates span ~$176B to $800B+ depending on whether services and adtech are bundled; the ~$200B "software spend" figure (Gartner-derived) is the most decision-useful for this theme. Treat any single TAM/CAGR number skeptically—most come from commercial research vendors, not primary sources.
- **ARR ≠ recognized revenue.** Consumption-model ARR (Agentforce, Breeze credits, monday AI ARR) runs one to two quarters ahead of GAAP revenue and can be lumpier; early adoption spikes may reflect experimentation budgets, not durable run-rate. Note that Salesforce's headline $3.4B "AI + data" ARR includes ~$1.1B Informatica Cloud, so stripped-out Agentforce is $1.2B.
- **The "SaaSpocalypse" magnitude is partly media-driven.** Sourced figures for value wiped ($285B, $1T, $2T) come largely from secondary/blog sources and vary widely; the *direction* (severe, AI-driven repricing) is corroborated by Morningstar moat downgrades and JPMorgan/Jefferies commentary, but exact magnitudes are approximate.
- **Fast-moving inputs.** Pricing models, restructurings, and leadership change quarter-to-quarter (Salesforce shipped three Agentforce pricing models in ~18 months; Adobe has simultaneous CEO and CFO transitions). This is a Summer 2026 snapshot and should be refreshed each quarter.
- **Watchlist framing only.** Company notes are theme-level context, not stock scores; individual scoring belongs to the later rubric phase.
